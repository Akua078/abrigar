## The Recompense of Mada K'ar

    Accounts tell of a group of adventurers who were drawn together by an anonymous summons, each guided to the same out-of-the-way tavern southeast of Fratera. There, they met a man called Kristoff Kringle, who claimed to serve Qani-Uma, an exiled power opposed to Father Winter. Through a projected vision, Qani-Uma revealed what he insisted was the truth: that Father Winter, known to many as a benevolent traveler, is in fact Mada-K’ar the Grandsmith, ruler of the hidden realm of Yulheim and master of an enforced, joyless winter.

    The adventurers were told that Mada-K’ar’s control depended on powerful artifacts called the Ember-Keys of Everywhere, which bound hearths, chimneys, and planar paths to his will. To reach Yulheim and challenge this rule, they would first need the Permafrost Globe, an ancient relic said to hold a perfect miniature of the fortress realm within it.

    Their journey took them far north into corrupted wilderness, to a frozen tomb known as the Ossuary of the First Frost. There, beneath twisted white trees and un-melting ice, the adventurers fought through undead guardians and confronted a spectral Portal Keeper sworn to bar all intruders. When the guardian fell, the Permafrost Globe was claimed.

    With the globe in hand, the group crossed the Forgotten Peaks to an ancient obsidian arch standing alone at the summit. Placing the globe upon its plinth caused it to dissolve into raw magic, tearing open a gateway and revealing Yulheim beyond.

    The adventurers stepped through into a silent village buried in perfect snow. Warm light glowed from empty homes, golden-inked lists hung in careful order, and the air carried the distant sound of relentless labor. Beyond the village rose the Grand Forge, where Mada-K’ar’s power was said to be made and maintained.

    What followed is unclear. Some say the adventurers marched on the forge to confront the False Father himself. Others claim they sought only to steal one of the Ember-Keys and break his grip on the world. What is known is that, afterward, winter magic across the realms shifted. Hearths burned differently. Old spirits stirred. And the story of Father Winter has not sounded quite the same since.

    People still argue whether the adventurers were heroes, heretics, or something worse. But no one denies this: after their journey to Yulheim, winter no longer feels the same.
