## An Alternate Form of Magic

The Pale Weave, sometimes called the 'Second Weave' or the 'Unholy Weave' is essentially a second system of magic existing in parallel with the 'normal' weave. The name is misleading, merely a convention to identify this entity as a source of magic acting similarly to the weave, though they are quite distinct in internal nature. The Pale Weave is certainly more powerful of the two, but its use is far more dangerous and much less understood. Those who wield it often find themselves with horrific consequences to rectify their great reward. 

Where the 'normal' Weave is often seen as a plane of fabric or as a property of space, The Pale Weave is quite different. Those who use the Pale Weave sometimes report vivid hallucinations of deceased loved ones or of ruptures in the Earth, visions of the past or the future. In these dreams and visions, they sometimes see The Pale Weave itself as a large fountain of light erupting from the earth, glowing brilliantly in either pure white or a soft blue. The soft blue color is often reflected in skeletons and zombies reanimated by the Pale Weave, those bodies enraptured in a soft blue glow.

## True Souls

These are players, literally. When they inhabit a character, called a 'vessel' in this way, they may enter and interact with Abrigar. They exist purely in the pale weave as it makes its way through each and every fiber of every living being, pupperteering their chosen creatures like a horrific child playing with their toys. A True Soul may take any creature as a vessel, but only experienced or bold true souls attempt to inhabit the pale weave itself.  

Calling a True Soul a God is like calling a King a celebrity: Insulting, inaccurate, and potentially fatal depending on the mood of the being you have offended. Where Gods in Abrigar fall short of  true omnipotence, True Souls prevail. But, you knew that already, didn't you?

## The Warforged

Similar to the Weshlkind, Warforged are vessels constructed without the influence of a true soul. They don't inherently have sentience or consciousness, but for some reason, they seem to keep miraculously attaining them. What an odd coincidence.

## The Weshlkind

Body without Spirit, essentially. These creatures are created when a true soul, in creating a large population, neglects a large portion of that population, and then does not see fit to create their surroundings. To oversimplify, these poor souls are what is left of those faceless victims of an unknown fate, in most cases.

## Elementals, Skeletons, and the like

Elementals, Skeletons, Ghosts, Specters and more are examples of the exact opposite of the Warforged and the Weshlkind. These are spirits without body, faceless things lacking identity or form, existing only to serve a purpose and then vanish. They shouldn't be considered sentient, though if they truly are is a matter of furious academic debate.

## The Sea of Creation

The Sea of Creation is a small plane containing an endless black sand beach, overlooking an ocean bathed in permanent night. Below the waves, there is Abrigar, and those who drown find themselves flying. The answers to every question that ever was exist here, together, within the most powerful being in Abrigar.

## Punishment

An infinite sea of black sand, similar to The Sea of Creation. Nothing is here, nothing but the discarded bodies of four of The Seven. Only the most destructive of creatures are sent here to rot, far from Abrigar.
