## Table Rules

<div className="c4">

<div>
### Swimming

If a creature does not have a swimming speed, all movement must be used to tread water for one turn. This allows them to make a DC:10 STR or DEX save to keep their head above water. Heavy armor imposes disavantage on this roll, as does being encumbered by carrying weight. This does not allow them to move or change their position in the water.
</div>

<div>
### Desperate Measures

A character currently making death saving throws can voluntarily fail all death saving throws on their turn. In exchange, they can take their full turn, and spells do not require a spell slot to cast. After taking this turn, the character dies normally, under any conditions their cause of death inflicted.
</div>

<div>
### Water Buffs

Lightning Damage and Cold damage are doubled for any creature under the 'wet' condition. Half of any lightning damage done to a creature in water or on a water surface is done to all other creatures also on that water surface or in that water within 20 feet.
</div>

<div>
### Crit Coins

Upon a natural 20 roll on any d20 test a player is given a coin that can be redeemed at any time to reroll another d20 test. Only one coin may be held at a time, per player, per campaign. 
</div>

<div>
### Magic Pockets

Items in the party inventory are shared and do not need to be independently accessed. 
</div>

<div>
### Devastating Criticals

Critical hits on attack rolls do double of their maximum possible damage, after applying all additional effects.
</div>

<div>
### Poison Death Saves

The 'poisoned' condition causes disadvantage on death saving throws.
</div>

<div>
### Secret Death Saves

Death saving throws are made behind the DM screen, known only to the player making the save, and to the Dungeon Master.
</div>

</div>

## The Naval Ruleset

For 'Skeleton Crew', it became necessary to create a ruleset for naval combat, ships, and oceanic travel. This is that ruleset, updated when rebalance or clarification is needed. Below is a list of upgrades that can apply to ships as the rules dictate. These upgrades are not all the upgrades that exist, and more can be found all over the world.
</div>

</div>

<div className="c2">
<div>
    ### Basics

    Every ship has four components, being the Hull, Sails, Wheel, and Cannons. All Components can be attacked, damaged, and disabled or destroyed in some way, described below.

    On a standard, default ship the Hull has resistance to slashing and piercing. The Wheel also has resistance to slashing and piercing, and cannons have resistance to bludgeoning, slashing, and piercing. Sails on a default ship have resistance to bludgeoning but vulnerability to fire. 

    All ship components are not affected by status conditions unless an upgrade states otherwise. All ship components are immune to poison and psychic damage unless an upgrade specifies otherwise.
</div>
<div>
    ### Naval Resources

    There are four basic resources that a ship can hold, being rations, lumber, cannonballs, and canvas. Standard prices are as follows (per unit):

    Rations - 5sp
    Lumber - 2gp
    Cannonballs - 3gp
    Canvas - 2gp

    To repair a ship component, the creature makes a STR, DEX, or INT check (their choice), DC 10. If the creature is proficient with carpenter's tools or water vehicles, it adds its proficiency bonus to the roll. On a success, the component regains the appropriate hit points. Lumber will heal the hull for 2d10 hp, and Canvas will heal the sails for 3d10 hp. On a failure, the materials are not expended, and no hit points are restored. This DC can be upgraded by ship upgrades.

    A medium or large creature needs one unit of rations each day. A creature that does not eat gains one level of exhaustion every 24 hours.

    Cannonballs are expended during battle. One unit of cannonballs supplies 5 shots from a cannon, unless an upgrade states otherwise.
</div>
<div>
    ### Sailing

    A standard ship can travel 120 miles in a day. This speed is affected by weather and wind, and can vary. For example, bad weather could slow a ship by a percentage, and good wind could hasten it by a set amount. The amount (And whether it is a percentage or a numerical change) is determined by the cause of the effect. 

    At least one creature must be conscious and at the wheel to determine where the ship sails, otherwise the crew will have no control over their destination.

    Turning the wheel of a ship requires proficiency with water vehicles to do with any control. To turn the ship during combat a creature must expend 30 feet of movement by default. 
</div>
<div>
    ### Combat

    Standard cannonballs do 50 bludgeoning damage. It takes a bonus action to load a cannon, and an action to fire it. Upon firing, a creature should make a tanged weapon attack using STR or DEX. Cannons have a range of (300/1200) feet. If a player is proficient with water vehicles they may add their Proficiency Bonus to the attack roll. 

    Each component has its own AC, which are all upgradable either as a group or as individual components, as the upgrade describes

    If the hull reaches 0 hp the ship begins to sink. From this point on, it cannot be repaired by any normal means. In ten minutes, it will be completely submerged. 
</div>

<div>
    ### Damaged Components

    If the wheel is below 50% of its maximum hp, it requires double the normal movement to turn, and if the wheel is destroyed it cannot be used, and the ship will continue on its path in a straight line (assuming the ship can move)

    When sails take damage, they move the ship with less speed. The ship will move only at half speed if the sails are below half their maximum hit points, and the ship will not move via wind at all if the sails are at 0hp. This multiplier is taken into account after weather, usually. Some speed modifiers, such as an ocean current, may be added after these modifiers.

    Standard Cannons have 50 hp. When they reach 0 hp they can no longer fire. If they were already loaded, they explode and deal 2d4 fire damage to every creature within five feet, or half damage if a creature succeeds a DC:12 DEX save. A creature automatically this save if they are under the incapacitated or prone conditions. This explosion is capable of damaging other ship components provided they are in range.

    As stated in the combat section, the ship begins to sink when the hull reaches 0hp.
</div>

<div>
    ### Upgrades

    This is where the fun begins. Ship components can be upgraded at a shipyard, provided there is a shipwright available to sell or install them. Upgrades are carried in the inventory of a ship and can be swapped at a shipyard. Installed upgrades cannot normally be removed from a ship unless an upgrade says otherwise. Only upgrades that have not yet been installed may be recovered or transferred. 

    Upgrades have a rarity system. Some upgrades may be too complicated to install for a novice shipwright, and so each shipwright has a rank according to their level of expertise. These levels correspond as magical item rarity, in that a common shipwright can install common upgrades, an uncommon shipwright can install uncommon upgrades, and so on. 

    Ships have only a certain number of upgrade slots. This is noted on the ship's stat block. This number can be increased, though doing so is typically very expensive and eventually reaches a practical limit.  Ship upgrades occupy one upgrade slot unless otherwise stated. Component upgrades are installed directly onto a component and do not occupy an upgrade slot. Each component can have only one component upgrade unless an upgrade states otherwise. 

    Ship upgrades and component upgrades may be found, purchased, or even created. They act similarly to items in this way. Upgrades can be stored in the ship's inventory until installed, and can be swapped out into that inventory when unequipped by a shipwright, in the same way as it is equipped. 
</div>

</div>
