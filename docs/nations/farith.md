<div className="intro">
Farith is a medium-sized nation in the middle of the Abrigarian mainland. It stands as a mixing pot for all nations, and next to every culture can be found here. Mining takes place near the peaks, fishing and oceanic activities on the shore, farming in the grasslands, and tensions with Balbia towards the border. 

Farith can be described as a jack of all trades nation. It specializes in nothing, but has a great variety of services and goods to be found. Farith is nestled between the Woods, Balbia, Arutu, and the Peaks. Taking mostly grasslands, along with some deltas along the shore, its position and resources are nothing to scoff at. It makes most of its money, though, from the West Loop Route that passes through it.

For as much as Farith stands as a mixing ground for culture and ideas, it also boils in tension. The disinterested kings that rule from Brogkigh do little to help as borderline race wars erupt in the streets. Even 'high standing' citizens use generous donations to churches as political warfare, hiring peasants to attack churches. 
</div>

  <div>
  ## Emblem

  Farith's emblem is old, generally disliked in even its own nation. It was made hastily during the battles for Farithian independence, and stood especially against Balbia. Afterwards, the identity of the nation changed dramatically, but not enough of the government has cared enough to officially create a new emblem. The one that stands represents the gold coin with the golden ring in the center, remnants of a failed attempt to gain Mineran alliance, and golden spokes from it, representing the shining light that Farith was supposed to be to those seeking freedom.
  </div>

  <div>
  ## Geography

  Farith is a nation in the middle of Abrigar, just north of Arutu and the Forgotten Peaks. The Ixcatepec River runs into and ends in the middle of the nation, and it serves as a major economic artery for the mainland. Brogkigh, the capital, sits in the northeastern corner of the nation near the coastline. This spot was chosen before the war with Balbia, and was chosen because it was once in the middle of the nation. Now, the northern reaches have become Balbia, and the capital is left fairly exposed. 
  </div>
  <div>
  ## Origins

  Farith emerged later in Abrigar's history, as those who did not align with either the militarism of Arutu or the druidic lifestyle of the woods, who fled the religions of Balbia, or feared the capitalism of Minera, ran here. They stood together at first, establishing themselves as a great nation. Balbia worked to crush them down, taking a large portion of the mainland from them, and Droggor, their main port city. Despite the loss, Farith survived, and its people lived on to grow to hate both Balbia and one another. 
  </div>

  <div>
  ## Government 

  Farith is a monarchy, currently ruled by a King. A council of religious figures as well as influential persons stands below the King and acts on his behalf, but the King makes his decisions and laws without limit here. Even so, the land remains stable, most Kings preferring to spend time with their harem rather than with their desk.
  </div>

<div>
## International Relations

Farith has fairly neutral relations to most nations, but hates Balbia. They warred in the distant past, and Balbia took all the land that is now Balbian mainland from Farith. The exact nature of this war is a subject of furious debate, and which side rebelled against which is a topic that can ruin careers. Farith has a good relationship with The Woods to facilitate trade from the Ixcatepec, though not as good relations as Morgana does, and an agreement with Arutu for trade and travel to keep the Western Loop intact. In this way, Farith thrives on its neutral relations to most nations, and takes advantage to be the middleman for trade and travel through Abrigar. 
</div>

  ## Capital - Brogkigh

  Brogkigh is the center of the tensions in Farith. The city serves as a trading hub for the nation, as well as an essential stop on the way to Droggor and the northern sea, and so traders pour through every day. Politicians throw money around like arrows in a war, building churches to fire at their rival religions, hire those underneath them to squabble, and so much more. As a result, every street in Brogkigh is choked with temples, drowning in propaganda, and rife with crime. It presents itself as a lovely place to be, but underneath those forced, false smiles, are hundreds of deaths.
