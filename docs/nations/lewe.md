<div className="intro">
Lewe is a godforsaken wasteland, a land drenched in chaos, lawlessness, and unchecked crime. It is a place where the rule of law has long since been gutted and left to rot. The very soil seems to bleed history, torn apart by decades—if not centuries—of anarchy and bloodshed. There is no functioning central government to speak of; what pathetic remnants of authority remain are splintered, with each major city or region controlled by a self-proclaimed lord, warlord, or criminal overlord. These arrogant pricks vie for dominance through relentless warfare, backstabbing politics, and shady deals that would make even the vilest snake oil salesman blush.

The lack of unified leadership has left the land fractured and festering, an open wound where bandits, mercenaries, and fugitives thrive. Lewe has become a haven for every kind of degenerate scumbag imaginable—from seasoned cutthroats fleeing justice in neighboring realms to outlaw mages, black market dealers, and slavers who operate with impunity. To the common folk, Lewe is a nightmare given form, but to a criminal? It’s a paradise. No rules. No order. No questions asked.

Justice, if you can even call it that, is doled out by those with the most swords, gold, or ruthlessness. Cities are often more dangerous than the lawless wilds between them, with violent gangs and corrupt militias running amok. Survival depends not on morals, but on wit, force, and how well you can slit a throat in the dark.

In short, Lewe is not just a land of chaos—it is chaos, alive and breathing. And for those who thrive in the shadows, it’s the closest thing to freedom they’ll ever know.
</div>

  <div>
  ## Emblem

  Lewe's Emblem is simple, a black snake eating its own tail, surrounded by a red border. The snake represents anarchy and crime, a system of brutality that the people of Lewe see as both self-destructive and unending, infinite suffering that keeps the world rolling.  The red border represents the blood that is shed to keep this system afloat, the cost of the nation's insane form of freedom.  The Gold backdrop is often ignored completely, even left out of some versions, but it does represent the spoils of this system and the riches it brings to those strong enough to grasp them. No one knows who designed the emblem for Lewe, but it's present everywhere, and used as a symbol for both freedom and anarchy. 
  </div>

  <div>
  ## Geography

  Lewe is the southernmost tip of Abrigar, a peninsula next to The Seven's Inlet.  It's mostly forests and tundra, and the more southern you go, the colder it gets. Most people live in the north, near the coast, and almost none are found on the southern tip. Some say The Ice connects to the bottom of Lewe in the winter, making the gap walkable, but these rumors aren't confirmed to the public. 
  </div>
  <div>
  ## Origins

  Lewe formed as a refuge from the order forming across Abrigar. As nations solidified and governments gained power, Lewe's inhabitants fought any internal structure. The state is a land of anarchy, and its inhabitants often resort to violence to solve problems.
  </div>

  <div>
  ## Government 

  Lewe is a nation of anarchy, where most areas and cities are lorded over by occupying armies or powerful criminal organizations. Each city is lorded over by a family or individual, provided it isn't drowning in war at any given moment.
  </div>

## International Relations

Lewe's international relations are hairy, at best. With a lack of government, the state is difficult to bargain with. Often, the cities inside Lewe are negotiated individually by their respective lords. Overall, Lewe has a surprisingly positive relationship with most of Abrigar, if only because their lords are willing to do what the high profile kings and queens cannot.
</div>

  ## Capital - Kahema

  A city of danger, full of newly constructed buildings surrounded by ruins. The place burns at all hours of the day, anarchy reigning. It can hardly be considered a city, the streets rampant with crime and chaos. No-one lives here, the streets and buildings dedicated to battle. The only reason the place still exists is because of the dozens of crime bosses that rush to claim the city for themselves. Its status as 'Capital' is purely symbolic, reflecting the internal disarray of the nation. No lord manages to stake a claim on Kahema, and the second one tries, every other lord attacks, keeping the city in a chaotic neutral state.
</div>

<div>
  
  ## Grambe

  Grambe is a Lewen island far south of the mainland, beyond the reach of any government eager to tax or regulate its trade. Nearly every acre is devoted to grain, hops, or managed forests harvested to fuel the island's kilns. At its center stands an immense brewery, smoke pouring from its chimneys day and night as thousands of workers labor to produce an endless tide of ale. Every ship that leaves Grambe does so with a hold full of barrels.

  ## Keakau

  Teetering on the edge of chaos and commerce, Keakau is Lewe’s most “stable” city—though that’s less a badge of honor and more a sarcastic punchline. This raucous port town throbs with life, choked with sails and sin. Merchant ships offload exotic cargo by day, while by night the taverns erupt with brawling pirates, mercenaries on the run, and adventurers too drunk or too damned to care. Law here is more rumor than reality. Drunkards stagger through blood-slick streets, and barkeeps change more often than the tides—usually due to a sudden, messy death.

At the center of this glorious disaster sits Lord Bucket, a notorious sailor-turned-ruler with a reputation carved from violence and vinegar. Cursed by a forgotten sea god, his left arm and right leg are solid wood, grim reminders of the price he paid for power. But far from being slowed, he rules with savage charisma from his subterranean fortress: The Rock Ring, a brutal underground arena where steel and sorcery clash in near-constant combat. It's part coliseum, part blood-soaked church—where gladiators earn glory, gold, or a gory end.
