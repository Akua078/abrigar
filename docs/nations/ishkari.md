<div className="intro">
Ishkari is an island nation south of Eplem and Arutu, in the southern strait. The islands here are cold, damp, with grey days and cold water. Despite that, much grows on these islands, plants that thrive in the cold, fish that gather close to the rocky shorelines. As a result of that, fishermen have flocked to these islands for centuries, and their villages and families have grown to become the best navigators in all of Abrigar. It is said that one Ishkaran navigator is better than any diviner wizard, that they can predict the weather perfectly, and ride any ocean current to anywhere in the sea faster than the finest steed. For all their lack of polish, few can rival their effectiveness. 

The Ishkari worship Lee above all else, earning a reputation as almost cult-like among outsiders. Their relationship with the goddess is one of practicality and mutual benefit. Fishermen and sailors offer fish, food, and prayers from every corner of the sea. In return, Lee grants subtle guidance to her faithful, revealing hints of favorable currents, approaching storms, and where the sea's bounty may be found.
</div>

  <div>
  ## Emblem

  The emblem of Ishkari is meant to resemble a wooden wheel before a blue sea and a black sky. It is simple, direct, and practical, like the attitude of the Ishkaran people. The four sided cross represents the four tomes and their respective knowledge. 
  </div>

  <div>
  ## Geography

  Ishkari sits to the south of the world, pushed up against the Southern Ice. Their islands are mostly cold, rocky, and small, so their trade relies on the resources under the water. They take advantage of the many, small, scattered islands to fish constantly, stopping to resupply when needed, and so they maintain their economic control of fish, but also of ship repairs and practical equipment. 
  </div>
  <div>
  ## Origins

  Ishkari emerged naturally in the early history of Abrigar, long before any other oceanic nations formed properly. The beginnings were natural, fishermen and navigators followed fish to find these islands, clustered together with abundant fish, and made their home where the bounty was great. Worship of Lee followed, and the tomes began to form. Now, centuries later, they continue the traditions from ancient seafaring, navigational techniques millennia old, and families as aged as the ocean itself.   
  </div>

  <div>
  ## Government 

  Ishkari is governed by the Council of Four, the eldest navigators from each of the four great islands. Though often described as a republic by foreign scholars, the comparison is imperfect. Each lord earns their seat not through noble birth or popular election, but through a lifetime spent upon the sea, proving themselves as a captain, navigator, and steward of their people. They are the most respected of their craft. The Council directs and advises the fishermen of Ishkari, telling them where to sail, where to fish, where to whale, and where to avoid. 
  </div>

<div>
## International Relations

Ishkari is a rare nation that has a fairly good standing with most other nations. They remained neutral in the Koralian Feuds, and after the dust settled, they took in refugees and lent support to both sides in recovery. Their belief in having as little waste as possible applies to lives, as well, and they have earned a reputation as calm and reliable. As such, Ishkaran fisheries supply much of the world, even the distant mainland, with seafood. 
</div>

  ## Capital - The Ishkaran Quartet

  The Ishkaran capital is split into four towns, spread over the main island cluster. These towns are Dufa, Catuka, Merma, and Adda, to the west, north, east, and south respectively. Each town is watched over by a lord, and the tomes are kept separated in these towns, except for when the lords meet in the center island of Havn. Otherwise, the four cities exist as separate port towns, each with markets and shipyards and services of their own.
