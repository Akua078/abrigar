# The Everwinter Family

<div className="c_lrg_rgt">
<div>
    <figure className="nrmlImage">
        ![The Everwinters](/img/characters/theEverwinters.png)
    </figure> 
    [First appeared in 'Hammer of Destiny']
    
</div>
<div>

    The Everwinters are one of Balbia’s oldest noble families, their name once spoken with reverence throughout the halls of the Church of Bisu. For generations, they stood as exemplars of orthodox faith, tradition, and moral rigidity, wielding immense influence over doctrine, clergy, and regional governance. Their power was never flashy, but it was absolute in the quiet, grinding way institutions prefer.

    That influence has faded. Slowly, stubbornly, and without the dignity the Everwinters believe they deserve. Arial Everwinter was meant to be the answer to that decline.

    Unlike her forebears, Arial embodied compassion as much as conviction. She was devout without being cruel, principled without being blind, and widely believed to be the family’s long-awaited renewal. Where Abba represented doctrine, Arial represented mercy. Where the Everwinters had once demanded reverence, she inspired it. Many believed that through her, the family would reclaim both relevance and respect within the Church of Bisu. Arial’s sudden death shattered the Everwinters’ remaining certainty. With her passing went their clearest path forward, leaving behind grief, unanswered prayers, and a family once again trapped in the shadow of its own past.

    Years before her death, during one of her journeys into the Ice, Arial encountered a wounded snow leopard tabaxi. Rather than passing him by or consigning him to fate, she chose mercy. She saved him, sheltered him, and ultimately brought him into the Everwinter household. The tabaxi, Qidi, became not merely a ward, but family. His presence was a quiet reflection of Arial’s beliefs: that faith was proven through care, not authority. Adopted in life, not desperation, he stands as the final living echo of Arial’s vision — a reminder that mercy once lived in the Everwinter name, and perhaps still can.

## Abba Everwinter

Abba Everwinter is a man carved from discipline and devotion, the unyielding head of his family and the Church of Bisu’s most formidable steward in Balbia. Every word he speaks carries weight, every glance enforces order, and those who step out of line quickly learn that mercy is a luxury he seldom affords. Devout to the last breath, he interprets divine law with rigid exactitude, and while outsiders see only a stern noble enforcing tradition, whispers suggest a mind constantly weighing faith, family, and power—an impenetrable figure whose true intentions remain just beyond the grasp of all but the most daring observers.

## Qideyna Everwinter

Qideyna Everwinter is ever seen with a polite smile fixed carefully in place, a practiced expression that masks a constant undercurrent of strain. Devoted to her husband and the ideals of the Everwinter household, she bears Abba’s expectations in silence, her composure held together by routine and restraint rather than peace. Those who linger long enough notice the subtle tells: the tension in her posture, the way her hands linger too long in the garden soil, the meticulous care she pours into cultivating both her plants and her children. Gardening has become her refuge, a quiet attempt to nurture growth where she feels herself slowly unraveling, and while her smile rarely falters, it never quite reaches her eyes.

## Arial Everwinter

Arial Everwinter is remembered as the brightest star of her family, a cleric of Bisu whose faith was defined not by rigidity, but by quiet compassion. Devout and celibate by choice, she undertook a holy quest in service to her god and gave her life in its fulfillment, meeting her fate with a warmth and serenity that left a lasting mark on all who knew her. Where Abba embodied structure and discipline, Arial came to represent something gentler, a vision of faith rooted in kindness, acceptance, and renewal rather than strict adherence alone. In death, she became a symbol of beginnings rather than endings, her legacy softly guiding the Everwinter name toward an age shaped less by law and more by understanding.

## Qidi Everwinter {#qidi-everwinter}

Qidi Everwinter is a snow leopard tabaxi of extraordinary rarity, a living remnant of a lineage born from devotion and loss. Snow leopard tabaxi trace their origins to two explorers who perished seeking Bisu in the northern Ice, only to be reborn by the goddess herself as her closest disciples. Since then, their kind has endured through isolation and brutal tradition, meeting rarely, bearing kittens only to abandon them so that only the strongest survive. Qidi was raised by this silence. Left alone in the Ice, he survived seventeen years through instinct, endurance, and an unyielding will to live, until injury finally reduced him to waiting for death. He did not die. He was found by Arial Everwinter. To the faithful, Qidi’s survival and discovery were unmistakable signs of divine favor. Snow leopard tabaxi are revered among worshippers of Bisu, regarded with importance rivaling that of aasimars, and Qidi’s presence alone carried immense religious weight. When he was brought south and folded into the Everwinter household, his existence became a symbol. As the family’s influence waned beneath the pressure of rival noble houses, Abba Everwinter leveraged Qidi’s presence as proof that Bisu’s blessing had not abandoned them. Qidi was displayed gently but deliberately, a quiet testament to divine favor and institutional legitimacy. Qidi himself understood none of this. Raised far from politics, doctrine, or civilization, Qidi possesses little interest in status or symbolism. He accepts the comforts of his new life with easy contentment, enjoying warmth, food, and indulgence without question. His worldview remains instinctual rather than ideological. Titles, faith, and power mean little to him. What matters to Qidi is loyalty, survival, and the bond forged at the edge of death. That bond was Arial. To Qidi, Arial Everwinter was not merely his rescuer. She was the one who claimed him when the Ice had determined to be rid of him, and in doing so, earned his devotion in a way that required no explanation. His attachment to her was absolute, instinctive, and unshakable. He did not worship her as others worship gods; he followed her as one follows the center of the world. In his private understanding, Arial was his queen, his goddess, his everything. Arial, in turn, noticed. Burdened by expectation and doctrine, pressed inward by her father’s rigid structure, she found something unexpectedly sustaining in Qidi’s devotion. Where her life demanded sacrifice, Qidi asked only for her presence. Where faith imposed distance, his affection was simple and unconditional. Over time, against her vows and her carefully maintained identity, Arial grew attached. The celibate cleric of Bisu came to love, not in defiance of her faith, but in a way that quietly redefined it. Their bond remained unspoken and undefined, shaped more by closeness and mutual reliance than by words. Before every journey, Arial spoke to Qidi of death. She told him it was nothing but a delay, that Bisu would embrace her soul when the time came, just as she would one day embrace his. She spoke of endings as beginnings, of faith as continuity rather than loss. Qidi struggled to understand these concepts, but he never doubted her. He trusted her certainty because it was hers.

Qidi remained after Arial’s end, unchanged in the way only absolute devotion can be. He stayed true because she told him to, because she had always spoken of death as a delay rather than a conclusion. Before every journey, she assured him that Bisu would welcome her when the time came, just as she would one day welcome him. That death was not an ending, but a beginning. Qidi never fully understood her certainty, but he never doubted it, because it was hers. In her absence, he keeps an effigy close, a small raven plush carefully stuffed with her feathers and infused with her scent, something of her spirit lingering within. He tends to it constantly, shielding it with the same instinctive protectiveness he once offered her, fussing over it as one might a kit that was never meant to be. No harm is permitted to come to it. To Qidi, it is not a symbol. It is her. 

Qidi spends his days now bringing honor to the Everwinter name, to her name, like she and her parents felt so strongly about. He understands very little of what he means to these people, but Arial said it was important, and so he goes. He eventually brought enough gold to buy the supplies for a seance. Supported by his mother, Qideyna Everwinter, he contacted Arial. Arial joined Qidi to guide him, and to show him the world she saw.
